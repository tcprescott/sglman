## The Watch page

:::snippet watch-page
The [Watch page](/live) is where to follow the event online. It's **Watch** in
the sidebar, it needs no account and no sign-in, so it's also the link to share
with friends, put in a tweet, or hang on a QR code at the venue. Someone who
reaches the community's join page without being a member gets a **Watch live**
button pointing at it.

It has:

- **On stream today**: a card per stage, with the match on now marked
  {chip:live:On now}, the next three after it, and the approved commentators
  and trackers
- **Coming up on stream**: every streamed match over the next week, by day
- **Tournaments**: each one's bracket and rules links and how far along it is
- **Get involved**, which the community will fill in
:::

![The Watch page on a phone: three stage cards, each with a Watch stream button and a LIVE match under On now, two of them with the next match under Up next, then a list of brackets](/static/help/on-air/watch-page-phone.webp "The Watch page, signed out.")

While the community picks a design, the page comes in three layouts: one long page, tabs, or the player with a rail beside it. You start on one at random. Switch with the buttons under the page title, and use the thumbs to say which you like. Your browser remembers both.

The page updates by itself as matches change. A match with no stage isn't on
it; find those on the Schedule view of the **Event** tab.

If you can run the match on the admin Schedule board, its ⋮ menu has **Open in
Schedule**, which opens the board on just that match.

## Finding the stream

Where a stage streams on Twitch, **Watch** on its card plays the stream right on
the page, above the stage cards, with the match that's on now beside it. That
stage's button reads **Watching** while it plays. It starts muted, since
browsers won't autoplay with sound, so unmute it in the player. Pressing
**Watch** on another stage switches the player over, and **Close player** stops
it. On a phone the player stays pinned to the top while you scroll. **Open on
Twitch** takes you to the channel, or to the Twitch app on a phone.

Nothing loads from Twitch until you press **Watch**. Once it plays, Twitch's
own player and cookies apply.

A stage streamed somewhere other than Twitch gets an **Open stream** link that
opens the stream in a new tab. A stage with no link isn't being streamed.

## When it's busy

When a lot of people arrive at once, staff can switch the page to a lighter
version, or it switches by itself. It looks the same and the player keeps
playing, but it checks for changes once a minute instead of the moment they
happen, so a match can take up to a minute to move to On now.

## Watching a match

Watching a match is a different thing from the Watch page. It gets you a Discord
DM whenever one particular match changes state, without you having to keep
anything open. It's for matches you're *not* in; if you're a player or approved
crew, you're told anyway.

See [Reading the schedule board](/help/schedule-board#watching-a-match) for how
to turn it on and off.

## Watching in person

Whether you can watch a match from the room it's played in, and what's expected
of you if you can, is the event's call rather than the app's. Some events keep
the tournament room to players and crew and point everyone else at the stream.
Where your community publishes its rules, they're under **Event Guide** in
the sidebar.

A stage is separate again: it belongs to the broadcast techs, not to a proctor,
and they set who can be where. Follow what the techs there tell you.
