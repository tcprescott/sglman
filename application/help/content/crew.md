Crew is the word for the two jobs that support a streamed match.

- A **commentator** talks over the match on stream.
- A **tracker** runs the item and progress tracking that viewers see alongside
  the game: the overlay showing what each player has found and where they're up
  to.

Both sign up the same way and follow the same path: sign up, get approved,
confirm, turn up. Anyone in the community can do it. You don't need a volunteer
role.

## Signing up

:::snippet crew-signup
Open **Event**, stay on the **Schedule** view, and turn on **Needs crew**. That
hides every match that's already covered or already under way, which on a busy
day is most of the board. Then tap **Sign up** in the match's
**Commentators** or **Trackers** column. On a phone each role sits under the
match's name with its own button, **Sign up as commentator** or **Sign up as
tracker**.

There's no confirmation step. If you tapped the wrong one, **Withdraw** is in
the same place.

Signing up is a request, not a booking. It tells staff you're available for that
slot; a staff member decides who actually gets it.

**Every streamed match has commentary.** Trackers are the role some tournaments
don't use. Where that's the case the **Trackers** column is left empty,
and that's deliberate rather than a fault.
:::

![A match on the board on a phone: Player Four vs Player One with no time yet, Commentary 0/1 and Tracking 0/1 both marked needs crew, each with a Sign up button](/static/help/crew/signup-phone.webp "A match that still needs crew, on a phone.")

When you can't sign up, the **Sign up** button is greyed out and says why
beside it:

- **You're playing** means it's your own match. You can't crew your own game.
- **Signups closed** means the match has started. Signup closes once a proctor
  starts it. A match that's only checked in is still open, because that's
  exactly when a stream finds out it's short a commentator.

If you're already signed up for that role on that match, you'll see
**Withdraw** in its place.

## Signing up from Discord

If you follow a tournament, the DM announcing each new match carries **Sign up
as Commentator** and **Sign up as Tracker** buttons. Pressing one does exactly
what **Sign up** on the board does. The DM's **View the schedule** button opens
the board if you want to see who's playing first.

You choose which tournaments DM you, and how much, under **Profile** →
*Notifications* → **Tournaments you follow**.
[Notifications and Discord DMs](/help/notifications#where-your-settings-live)
explains the levels. **Streamed, plus possible streams** is the one most crew want: it
covers matches with a stage and the ones staff are still thinking about
streaming.

## Getting approved

:::snippet crew-approval
**Staff have the final say on every crew assignment.** A staff member, the
tournament's admin, or its crew coordinator decides who covers each slot.
Signing up puts you forward; it doesn't reserve anything.

Approval isn't automatic and isn't first come, first served. Staff also see what
else you're committed to at that time, including matches you're playing in, so a
clash is usually why a signup gets passed over.

Staff get a Discord DM the moment you sign up. When they approve you, Wizzrobe
DMs you too, with an **Acknowledge** button and a **View your crew slot** button
that opens your slot on My Schedule.

Staff can also put you on a match you never signed up for. You get the same DM,
and the slot shows up on My Schedule ready to confirm.

They can **withdraw an approval** they already gave, up to and including the day
of the match, if the shape of the stream changes or someone better suited
becomes free. You get a DM when that happens, your confirmation is cleared, and
you're back to waiting for approval. If they take you off the
match altogether, it disappears from your list. Neither is a judgement on you.
:::

## Confirming

:::snippet crew-confirm
Once you're approved you still have to **confirm** that you can cover the slot.
Until you do, staff can't tell whether you've seen the approval.

You can confirm from three places, and they all do the same thing:

- **Confirm**, next to **Withdraw** in your role's column on My Schedule
- the same **Confirm** on the schedule board
- **Acknowledge**, on the DM you were sent
:::

## My Schedule

**Crew you signed up for**, on Home's **My Schedule** tab, is the schedule
board narrowed to the matches you crew. If you aren't playing in any tournament
it's the first thing on the tab; if you are, it comes after your own matches.
Each line reads the way it does on the schedule, with your own **Withdraw**
(and **Confirm**, once you're approved) in your role's column, so you can tell
where you stand from the names:

:::snippet crew-status
| Beside your name | What it means |
|---|---|
| A clock, *waiting for approval* | You've signed up. Staff haven't decided yet. |
| A tick, with **Confirm** in your tray | You have the slot. Confirm that you can still cover it. |
| A tick, and no **Confirm** | You have the slot and you've confirmed. Nothing left to do. |

Finished matches drop off. Add *Finished* and *Confirmed* to the **State**
filter to see the ones you've already worked.
:::

## Reading the crew columns

The board has a **Commentators** and a **Trackers** column. Each starts with
approved crew against what the match needs: "1/2" means one approved
commentator out of two. Someone still waiting for approval doesn't count, so a
match can have three signups and still say "needs crew". **Needs crew** uses
the same rule when it decides whether a match is covered. On a phone each card
names the role instead ("Commentary 1/2").

Your own **Sign up** or **Withdraw** sits in a shaded box under the count, in
the same spot on every match. When there's nothing you can do, the button is
greyed out and says why, such as "Signups closed" or "You're playing".

Under that are the names: a tick for
approved, a clock and "waiting for approval" for everyone else, and **You** for
you. "No signups yet" means nobody has asked for that slot.

## Withdrawing

:::snippet crew-withdraw
**Withdraw** takes you off a slot. You can do it from My Schedule or from the
schedule board, before or after approval, and even once the match has started.

The two aren't the same act. Withdrawing from a slot nobody has approved yet
costs no one anything. Withdrawing from one you were approved for means staff
have to find cover, so they're told straight away. The app says so before you
confirm.

Withdrawing after you'd already confirmed also clears your confirmation. Do it
as early as you can.
:::

## If nobody approves you

Signups wait for approval until staff work through them, which at a busy event
may not be until shortly before the match. If a match you signed up for has
started and you never heard back, the slot went to someone else. You can
withdraw the stale signup or leave it; either way it drops off your list once
the match ends.

## On the day

Where to be and when, who runs the countdown, and how a stage works are the
event's to say rather than the app's. Where your community publishes them,
they're under **Event Information** in the sidebar.
